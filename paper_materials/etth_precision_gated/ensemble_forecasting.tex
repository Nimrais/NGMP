\subsection{Ensemble Forecasting}

We consider univariate long-horizon forecasting on the ETTh1 and ETTh2
electricity-transformer data sets, whose observations are hourly.  From the
preceding 96-hour multivariate context, the task is to predict one standardized
oil-temperature target (\texttt{OT}) $H\in\{96,192,336,720\}$ hours ahead.
Five independently trained neural forecasters (CNN, NLinear, LSTM, DLinear,
and NConv) provide candidate predictions.  Constant forecasts at the 10th and
90th training-set quantiles complete the frozen seven-expert bank.  The gate is
fitted on the original validation partition and evaluated on the original test
partition; no expert or VAE representation is retrained.

The controlled edge-uncertainty experiments in \cref{sec:edge-uncertainty}
predict a larger separation in NLL than in point error when
uncertain reliability updates are composed.  We test that prediction against
four neural gates and a precision-gated probabilistic ensemble.  The neural
baselines are an affine gate and a two-layer ReLU gate trained with Adam, and
an affine gate and two-layer ReLU gate trained with IVON
\citep{shen_variational_2024}. Here $K=1000$ means that the IVON posterior
predictive is approximated with 1{,}000 gate-weight draws. Each draw produces
one Gaussian ensemble component; the reported NLL uses the equally weighted
mixture density, and the point forecast averages the component means.
The Adam and IVON rows are independently trained neural benchmarks; no causal
optimizer effect is inferred from their contrast.  For readability, the main
table retains the stronger IVON neural baselines; all Adam results remain in
the appendix.

The affine IVON gate is the closest neural comparison to the precision-gated
model: both consume the same 65-dimensional context and frozen seven-expert
bank, and both map context affinely to seven reliability scores.  Their scale
semantics differ.  IVON optimizes the softmax-weighted expert squared error;
adding a common shift to all seven logits leaves that objective unchanged, so
the precision scale implied by those logits is unidentified.  NGMP instead
performs inference in an explicit probabilistic precision model whose priors
and likelihood determine both relative weights and absolute predictive scale.

\paragraph{Pre-existing NGMP result.}
We report the existing affine independent-expert \texttt{linear2\_ngmp}
configuration, fitted on the complete original validation partition at every
horizon.  The same model and hyperparameters are used in all eight cells; no
per-cell test-result selection is performed.  These outcomes were inspected
in earlier exploratory work, so they are retrospective results rather than an
evaluation on a historically unseen test set.

The Dynamic precision-gated ensemble of
\citet{lukashchuk_composing_2026} supplies the model-family provenance, frozen
experts, and historical 2026 PVMP results.  We report that historical
row for continuity.  Its configuration differs from the affine NGMP model, so
their contrast is not a matched inference comparison and cannot
attribute differences to inference alone.

\paragraph{Results.}
Across the benchmark, RMSE alone often makes the neural gates appear
competitive, whereas their NLL is substantially worse than that of both
precision-gated models. This larger separation in NLL than in RMSE supports
the prediction from the controlled experiments. Against historical PVMP,
affine NGMP has lower NLL at all
four ETTh1 horizons and at ETTh2 horizons 192 and 336.  It has lower RMSE at
ETTh1 horizons 96, 192, and 720 and at ETTh2 horizons 96, 192, and 336.  Normal
confidence intervals are shown with every point estimate.  Full results,
including the Adam gates omitted from the compact main table, are reported in
\cref{tab:etth-precision-gated-full,tab:etth-precision-gated-full-etth2}.
